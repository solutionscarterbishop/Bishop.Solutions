About

The short story of a long-standing habit: build the thing that makes the work easier.

I'm Carter Bishop. I've spent the last several years working in insurance across 40+ states, learning how a real, high-friction industry actually operates — and then building the software, systems, and marketing infrastructure to make it move faster. This page is the narrative behind the work.

Where it started

Everything I do professionally comes from a straightforward foundation: I like working with people, and I like watching systems work well. My first jobs were customer-facing — Men's Wearhouse, Panera Bread, the kind of roles where you learn quickly whether an operation is set up to make employees successful or set up to fight them.

Watching operations up close for years taught me one thing that shaped everything after: most business problems are workflow problems in disguise. The software and processes people use every day either amplify their effort or drain it. That observation would end up defining my career.

The insurance chapter

When I moved into insurance as a licensed Life & Health agent, I got the same view from the inside of a much bigger, much more regulated industry. I worked with clients across 40+ states and became appointed with 40+ carriers, focused primarily on Medicare, life, and supplemental products. Insurance is a business of trust, timing, and paperwork — and I learned the shape of the workflows the hard way, one call and one application at a time.

Two things became obvious. First, the client experience matters more than any single product feature — a well-run brokerage feels different from a sloppy one, and clients can tell. Second, the tools most agents use are, honestly, not very good. Fragmented CRMs, spreadsheets patched together, compliance concerns handled by memory. The gap between what agents needed and what they had was enormous.

The pivot into building

So I started building. The first version of what became Bishop CRM was a personal tool: a place to keep contacts, pipeline, and follow-ups without paying for five overlapping subscriptions. It grew because the problems it solved were problems every operator I talked to also had — the power dialer, unified inbox, TCPA-safe communication, commission tracking, calendars, reporting. I kept building because there was always another rough edge worth smoothing.

Around the same time, I started applying the same discipline elsewhere. Review Intelligence came out of a simple question: what would it take to actually understand what 150 locations of a business are hearing from their customers? The answer turned into a platform that analyzes 73,000+ reviews with sentiment, complaint classification, and revenue-opportunity detection. Marketing Command Center came out of the same operator instinct — real marketing teams don't need more tools, they need one clean loop from planning to publishing to performance.

How I think

Understand the business. Map the workflow. Remove the friction. Ship the tool. Measure it in the real world.

That's the loop. It's the same discipline whether the surface is insurance, marketing, review analytics, or an internal ops dashboard. I'd rather ship one boring internal tool that quietly replaces three vendors than pitch a "revolutionary" platform. The best software is the software you don't notice because the business just works.

I combine a few things that don't usually sit together: real customer-facing work, real regulated-industry experience, and enough engineering fluency to actually build the systems instead of writing about them. That's the perspective I bring to every project.

What I care about

  • Execution. Working software beats a great deck every time.
  • Ownership. If it's on the roadmap, someone's name is on it — usually mine.
  • Systems thinking. Every workflow has a shape. Understand the shape before you touch the code.
  • Reliability. The tools businesses live on should be the ones they think about the least.
  • Being useful. If a project doesn't make someone's day meaningfully easier, it's not done yet.

Where I'm going

Bishop Solutions is the long-term home for everything I build — the projects, the writing, and the consulting engagements that fit. I want it to remain a place where a recruiter, a founder, a business owner, or an operator can land, understand quickly who I am and what I've done, and reach out if it makes sense to work together.

If you're building something interesting — inside insurance, adjacent to it, or completely outside — say hello.
