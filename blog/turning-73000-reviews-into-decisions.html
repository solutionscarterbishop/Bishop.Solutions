Turning 73,000 reviews into decisions, not dashboards

Multi-location businesses drown in review data and starve for insight. The fix isn't a prettier dashboard — it's a change in what you're measuring, how you roll it up, and what you hand the operator on a Monday morning.

What we started with

150+ locations. 73,000+ pieces of customer feedback. A patchwork of dashboards nobody trusted. Aggregate star ratings drifted so slowly that by the time a location's average moved, the underlying problem had already cost real revenue.

Where averages fail

Aggregate ratings hide the two things operators actually need to know:

  • What changed? A single complaint category can spike in one week without moving the star average at all.
  • Where should attention go this week? An operator with 30 stores in their region can visit two. Which two?

What we built instead

Sentence-level sentiment classification. A complaint taxonomy shaped by real operator experience, not by NLP defaults. Rollups at brand, region, and location levels. Movement detection that surfaces changes before the average catches up. A weekly one-page brief per location that says: here's what changed, here's what to do.

Averages describe the past. Movement describes the present.

Design choices that mattered

  1. Taxonomy is the product. The complaint categories the team commits to determine every downstream decision. Get them right.
  2. Sentence-level, not review-level. Reviews are messy blends. Sentence-level classification captures the multiple things a customer actually said.
  3. Alerts, not dashboards. Operators don't open dashboards; they open their phones. Send the brief.
  4. Explainability. Every rollup number can be clicked back to the sentences that made it up. Trust follows.

The operator side

The most valuable output isn't a chart. It's the one-page brief that lands every Monday for each location: three things that changed, one thing to try this week, and a small handful of individual quotes that made the difference. District managers stopped scrolling dashboards and started running the brief as a meeting agenda.

What this unlocks

Cycle time between customer feedback and operational response drops from weeks to days. Small complaint categories get killed while they're still small. Cross-location learning happens because the taxonomy makes it comparable.
