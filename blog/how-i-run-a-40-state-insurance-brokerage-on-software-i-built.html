How I run a 40-state insurance brokerage on software I built

Bishop Plans covers Medicare, life, and supplemental insurance across 40+ states and 40+ carriers. The operation runs on internal software I designed and built. Here's what that stack looks like and why it turned out to be the most important operational decision I've made.

The stack

Three internal platforms carry most of the load:

  • Bishop CRM — the operational spine. Contacts, pipeline, dialer, unified inbox, TCPA compliance, commissions, and reporting.
  • Marketing Command Center — planning, content, approvals, publishing, compliance, and performance.
  • SOP Studio — the documented, executable playbook for how work actually gets done.

Around them sit the vendor tools that are cheaper to rent than to build — carrier portals, e-app tools, telephony carriers, calendar and video.

Why building was the right call

Insurance is a business of trust, timing, and paperwork. The industry-standard toolchain is neither trustworthy (data lives in the vendor), nor timely (handoffs everywhere), nor a good match for the paperwork (workflows leak through the cracks). Building the tools that fit the actual workflow was cheaper than fighting the ones that don't.

The operational leverage isn't the code. It's the fit.

What changed in the day

  • The pipeline is real. Every contact record has an owner, a next action, and a live conversation history.
  • Compliance is ambient. TCPA consent and suppression live in the messaging layer, so the risky path is harder than the safe path.
  • Commissions are live. No end-of-month reconciliation. The dashboard is the source of truth.
  • Marketing is a loop. Content goes out, performance comes back, next campaign is informed. Nothing gets lost between departments because there aren't departments.
  • Post-enrollment service is a system, not a favor. Every client interaction is logged, searchable, and revivable years later.

What the stack quietly enables

Multi-state operation. Consistent client experience regardless of geography. Fast onboarding of new agents when the time comes. A brokerage that can run without me being the bottleneck.

What I wouldn't build

Anything commodity. Telephony carriers, e-signature, calendar, video, most infrastructure. Rent the commodity, build the advantage.
