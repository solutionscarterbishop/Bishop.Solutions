Practical AI automation for small teams

Skip the hype cycle. Here's a boring, useful framework for finding the two or three places AI actually deserves to live inside a working business.

The trap

Most small teams either avoid AI entirely (losing real leverage) or shove it into every corner (adding noise and risk). Both patterns come from the same mistake: treating AI as a feature to adopt instead of a capability to place carefully.

Where AI earns its keep

Three job shapes. If your workflow has any of these, AI is worth wiring in.

  • Repetitive classification. Sorting inbound into buckets — leads by intent, tickets by urgency, reviews by category, transactions by GL code. AI does this well and reliably.
  • First-draft generation. The 60% draft you'll rewrite anyway — outreach, summaries, code scaffolds, briefs. AI is a leverage multiplier for humans who edit.
  • Structured extraction. Pulling fields from unstructured input — receipts, contracts, applications, transcripts. Reliable, checkable, high ROI.

Where it doesn't belong (yet)

  • Anywhere a wrong answer is expensive and hard to catch.
  • Anywhere the workflow already runs cleanly on a deterministic rule.
  • Anywhere you can't measure whether the output was any good.
If you can't measure the output, you can't automate the input.

How to place it

  1. Pick one workflow with a job shape above.
  2. Define what "good" looks like as a small evaluation set of past examples.
  3. Wire the AI step in behind a human review gate.
  4. Measure precision and recall against the evaluation set for two weeks.
  5. Only then let it run unattended for the class of tasks it's proven on.

The stack I actually reach for

A frontier LLM behind a clean API, a router that picks the right model per job, prompt templates versioned like code, a small evaluation harness, and observability that logs inputs, outputs, and human overrides. That's the entire spine.
